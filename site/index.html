The challenge

Bringing real-world financial assets onto blockchain requires far more than token issuance. It means building infrastructure that connects financial products, legal ownership, investor eligibility, compliance, trading, settlement and asset servicing, while making the underlying technology accessible and usable.

Over more than eight years, I led the evolution of Swarm's platform across these interconnected layers, translating financial and regulatory requirements into product architecture and working software.

My role

As Head of Product, I led product strategy, platform architecture and delivery across the ecosystem. Working at the intersection of financial products, regulatory requirements and engineering, I translated complex business and compliance needs into technical direction, product priorities and implementation.

My role spanned defining what the platform needed to do, how its components fit together, and leading the engineering effort responsible for delivering it.

The result

An integrated digital-asset platform spanning regulated trading, tokenised real-world assets, cross-chain infrastructure, data, programmable financial flows and compliance, bringing together the product and engineering foundations required to operate digital securities on blockchain infrastructure.

How a build runs

AI makes writing code fast. The expensive mistakes are still the decisions, so those come first.

  1. Decide first

    We settle what is costly to change later before any code exists: who it is for, what is left out, and what done means.

  2. Prove the risky parts

    Every outside system gets a small working test call before I build screens on top of it.

  3. Build to the agreed scope

    I direct AI coding tools to write the software and I check what they produce. You see it working early.

  4. Ship and hand over

    You get working software and the record of decisions behind it.

How I actually work. I direct AI coding tools; I don't hand-write every line. What you pay for is product judgement, architecture, and knowing what to check.
